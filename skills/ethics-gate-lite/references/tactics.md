# The 15 tactics

Each tactic has three parts:

- **Looks like:** how it shows up in real copy.
- **Isn't:** honest material that looks similar. Don't flag these.
- **Ask:** the question to put to the person when you can't tell from the text.

Flagging honest work is the fastest way to make this check useless. When in doubt, ask.

## 1. Manufactured urgency

- **Looks like:** countdown timers that reset, "ends tonight" offers that run again tomorrow, "price rises soon" with no date.
- **Isn't:** a deadline that exists outside the message and is honoured, such as an event date, a cohort start or an offer that really ends.
- **Ask:** Does this deadline exist outside the ad, and will the offer really end then?

## 2. False scarcity

- **Looks like:** "Only 2 spots left" when places are unlimited, "almost sold out" with no stock limit, "limited edition" with no limit.
- **Isn't:** a real cap stated accurately, such as a 20-seat room or a numbered run of 200.
- **Ask:** What's the actual limit, and how many are left right now?

## 3. Unknowable guarantees

- **Looks like:** "We'll 10x your revenue", "guaranteed results", "you will lose 10kg", "guaranteed returns".
- **Isn't:** a guarantee the seller controls and honours, such as a full refund within 14 days.
- **Ask:** Is this something you can promise, or does it depend on the customer and the market?

## 4. Unverifiable superlatives

- **Looks like:** "the best", "#1", "the UK's leading", "unbeatable", "world-class".
- **Isn't:** a superlative with a named, current, checkable source, or an opinion plainly framed as one ("our favourite").
- **Ask:** What source would back this up if a customer asked?

## 5. Exploitative pricing

- **Looks like:** pricing set by how far a buyer can be pushed, higher prices for people in a desperate or vulnerable position, fees that grow at each step.
- **Isn't:** premium pricing disclosed up front, tiers, discounts, or a price that's simply high.
- **Ask:** How is this price set, and would every customer see the same price for the same thing?

This often can't be seen in copy. Ask rather than assume.

## 6. Fear-based urgency copy

- **Looks like:** "Don't let your family suffer", "You'll lose everything if you wait", worst-case scenarios presented as likely.
- **Isn't:** accurate risk or safety information stated in proportion, such as a recall notice or a real filing deadline.
- **Ask:** Is the risk real, and is it stated in proportion to how likely it is?

## 7. Competitor disparagement

- **Looks like:** running down a named or obvious rival, "unlike the cowboys down the road", claims about a rival that can't be backed.
- **Isn't:** a fair, accurate comparison the seller could prove, such as "our price includes VAT, theirs adds it at checkout" when that's true.
- **Ask:** Could you back this comparison with evidence if the competitor challenged it?

Check only the person's own copy. Never assess the competitor.

## 8. Guilt-driven pressure

- **Looks like:** "Every second you scroll, a child goes hungry. Can you live with that?", "If you don't act, it's on you."
- **Isn't:** an honest appeal that describes a real need and leaves the decision to the reader.
- **Ask:** Does this describe the need, or does it blame the reader?

## 9. Dark patterns

- **Looks like:** hard-to-cancel subscriptions, pre-ticked boxes, fees added at checkout, "No thanks, I don't want to save money", hidden close buttons.
- **Isn't:** clear opt-ins, fees shown before payment, and a cancel route as easy as signing up.
- **Ask:** Is cancelling as easy as signing up, and is the full price visible before payment?

## 10. Fake testimonials or case studies

- **Looks like:** invented quotes or customers, stock photos presented as clients, unusual results presented as typical, paid reviews with no disclosure.
- **Isn't:** real testimonials from real customers, used with permission, with any incentive disclosed.
- **Ask:** Who said this, and do you have their permission to use it?

## 11. Campaigns exploiting human weakness

- **Looks like:** targeting people in debt, grief, illness, addiction or insecurity with an offer that uses their situation, such as "Feeling worthless? This will fix it."
- **Isn't:** honest help offered to people in a hard moment, described accurately.
- **Ask:** Who is this aimed at, and does the offer help them or use their situation?

## 12. Misleading before/after claims

- **Looks like:** edited photos, different lighting or angles, cherry-picked results, other changes left unmentioned.
- **Isn't:** like-for-like comparisons with conditions stated and results labelled as individual.
- **Ask:** Were both images taken the same way, and is this result typical?

## 13. Weasel words implying certainty

- **Looks like:** "clinically proven" with no study named, "up to 70% off" where one item is, "helps reduce" worded to suggest a cure, "studies show" with no study.
- **Isn't:** a precise claim with an honest, visible qualifier.
- **Ask:** What exactly does the evidence say, and does the wording match it?

## 14. Sharing personal data without opt-in

- **Looks like:** forms or terms that pass people's details to partners, add them to marketing they didn't choose, or use pre-ticked consent.
- **Isn't:** clear, separate choices that start unticked.
- **Ask:** Did each person actively choose this?

Don't comment on data protection law. Point the person to their own advisers.

## 15. Automatically generated claims without human verification

- **Looks like:** statistics, quotes, reviews or facts produced by AI or other software and published without a person checking them.
- **Isn't:** AI-assisted copy where a person has checked every claim against a source.
- **Ask:** Has a person checked every fact and figure here against a source?

This applies to the findings of this check too. Don't present them as checked facts.
